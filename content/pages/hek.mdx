Humrahe Khair, meaning Together for Good, is our platform for cross-community collaboration and shared service. It creates spaces where leaders, educators, NGO professionals and community members can meet, identify common challenges and develop cooperative responses together. Since January 2025 the programme has grown from local conversations into a wider network of gatherings and partnerships spanning India and the USA.

## The second annual conference: What are positive life outcomes?

Our first Humrahe Khair conference, in Thane in December 2025, explored what we do, the strengths we bring, and the gaps we face. The second annual conference takes the next step: how can our work support positive, lasting changes in the lives of individuals, families and communities? What are the challenges of holistic upliftment, and how do different parts of life connect? Alongside meeting immediate needs, we consider what helps individuals, families and communities sustain progress and improve their life outcomes and quality of life over time.

### A roundtable format

Delegates meet in small, curated groups, so that everyone has a greater opportunity to share, learn and connect. Each table brings together a contributing NGO leader, at least two other participants with experience in the same area, and participants from other fields. The table works through a real case, hears how each organisation approaches it, and prepares a one-page summary.

<CoverCard>
### Five questions for every table

1. What changes would matter, and how could progress be recognised?
2. What would help those changes last?
3. What have we learned from the organisations' different approaches?
4. What is missing or remains unresolved?
5. What useful next step could we suggest?
</CoverCard>

Facilitators then present each table's summary to a larger forum for moderated discussion and reflection, and the day closes with a presentation of the Community Atlas map.

## Vision

Humrahe Khair aims to be a trusted network in which people and organisations learn from one another, share ideas and resources, respond to needs, and strengthen their collective capacity to serve — honouring the distinct identities of participants while cultivating a shared ethic of human dignity, compassion and responsibility.

In a time when division is commonplace, Humrahe Khair unites people and organisations who believe in human dignity above all else — forming a collective force for good that closes gaps, shares resources, and walks alongside communities in service.

## Founder and philosophy

His Holiness Syedna Taher Fakhruddin Saheb, the 54th Dai al-Mutlaq, founded Humrahe Khair in 2025. He was personally trained in Islamic sciences, Fatimid philosophy and the heritage of his predecessors by his father, His Holiness Syedna Khuzaima Qutbuddin Saheb, founder of Zahra Hasanaat. With hands-on experience in finance and agricultural development, he founded and led a profitable hedge fund in the USA in the early 2000s and later built a commercially viable, industrial-scale fruit farming model in California. He brings renewed energy to the office of Dai al-Mutlaq, combining deep Islamic scholarship with an astute understanding of finance and trade.

Syedna Fakhruddin teaches that serving humanity is a sacred responsibility and is an advocate for "human solidarity". He encourages collaboration in humanitarian service and believes there is no dichotomy between preserving and respecting distinct identities and traditions, and living in harmony and helping each other. [Taqreeb](interfaith.html) is the foundation's programme for strengthening relationships within the wider intra-Muslim and interfaith communities and across civic communities, built on the belief that understanding grows through sincere conversation, respectful encounter and practical cooperation. Humrahe Khair emerged from this work as a focused platform for humanitarian collaboration.

Humrahe Khair brings together people and organisations dedicated to humanitarian service, creating opportunities to learn from one another, share ideas and resources, and strengthen collective impact — while identifying gaps, surfacing new opportunities to collaborate, and deepening mutual understanding.

## Faith, shared mission and human solidarity

Humrahe Khair is a platform for people and organisations committed to humanitarian service to meet, understand one another's work, learn across sectors and faiths, and identify opportunities for collective action. Its focus is not dialogue for dialogue's sake. It seeks to create the conditions in which dialogue can become relationship, and relationship can become service.

<MediaText image="/assets/uploads/zh/2026/11/hek-2025-panel.webp" alt="At the Humrahe Khair 2025 conference, a speaker smiles as he talks into a microphone at a panel of faith leaders seated beneath the Humrahe Khair logo" layout="wide" ratio="3/1" focus="50% 100%" caption="Faith leaders in conversation on the Humrahe Khair 2025 panel">
Humrahe Khair is grounded in the belief that people of different faith traditions share a responsibility to care for those in need. Syedna Taher Fakhruddin TUS's vision is to build upon that shared responsibility by creating opportunities for relationships, understanding and meaningful cooperation across communities. The initiative does not seek theological sameness. Rather, it recognises that distinct faith traditions can remain deeply rooted in their own beliefs while finding common ground in compassion, dignity, responsibility, generosity, justice and service.
</MediaText>

The goal is therefore to facilitate interaction. A faith leader may meet a grassroots organisation. A physician may learn from an educator. A philanthropist may encounter a small community initiative. An academic may connect research with lived experience. A nonprofit may discover another organisation with complementary expertise. These encounters can turn strangers into collaborators, and collaborators into long-term partners.

The 2025 Humrahe Khair conference described collaboration and inclusion not as optional additions to social change, but as essential drivers of it. Its focus on poverty and its root causes brought together work in food security, health, livelihoods, education and social cohesion. The aspiration was not only communal harmony, but what Syedna Taher Fakhruddin described as human solidarity: people working together for good because the needs of the human family call them to do so.

## What we do

### Dialogue gatherings

Structured gatherings bring together leaders, educators, NGO professionals and community members to identify shared challenges, map local resources and shape cooperative responses. Four major convenings took place between January 2025 and April 2026, in Utah, Boston and Mumbai.

### Collaborative service projects

We create opportunities for organisations and volunteers from different communities to work side by side on social service projects, building trust through practical action rather than conversation alone.

### The Community Atlas

A resource-mapping initiative that documents organisations, identifies gaps and highlights opportunities for collaboration across communities. A prototype is in development, alongside an organisational directory and supporting websites.

### Partnerships and networks

We build long-term partnerships with academic institutions, NGOs, civic groups and social-impact leaders, and connect participants to wider networks of expertise, organisations and resources.

### Focus groups and follow-up

From 2026 to 2027 the programme is launching focus groups in India and the USA and formalising volunteer and partnership pathways, so that each gathering leads to sustained cooperation rather than a one-off event.

## Unity through service

<MediaText image="/assets/uploads/zh/2026/11/hek-2025-harmony-prize-brief.webp" alt="A delegate at Humrahe Khair 2025 reads the brief for the Syedna Khuzaima Qutbuddin Harmony Prize 2026, which lists Harmony in Local Service, Innovation, Youth Leadership, Interfaith Collaboration and Lived Experience Leadership" layout="inset" ratio="1/1" focus="25% 50%">
The Syedna Khuzaima Qutbuddin Harmony Cohort extends this philosophy by recognising organisations for different expressions of unity through service — including local service, innovation, youth leadership, interfaith collaboration and lived-experience leadership. The recognition is deliberately non-competitive and non-hierarchical. Its purpose is to elevate good work, foster mutual learning, and create new opportunities for partnership.

This is consistent with the wider Humrahe Khair ethos: the aim is not to establish which organisation is best, but to ask what each organisation contributes, and what becomes possible when those strengths are connected.

## Our approach

Humrahe Khair follows a dual-focus strategy. In the short term, structured gatherings reduce isolation between communities and build trust between organisations; in the long term, the programme invests in durable infrastructure — resource mapping, volunteer networks and organisational directories — so that collaboration continues between events. Each convening feeds into practical follow-up, with participants moving from dialogue towards resource-sharing, joint projects and lasting partnerships, while every participating community keeps its own distinct identity and traditions.

## Areas of collaboration

Humrahe Khair begins with relationships and moves toward service. Its purpose is to help participants understand what others are doing, recognise complementary strengths, identify gaps, and discover where working together can improve outcomes.

### Health and well-being

Possible collaboration includes preventive-health education, access to care, maternal and child health, mental well-being, nutrition, disability support, community-based wellness, specialist referral networks, and coordinated support for families facing complex medical and financial burdens.

### Education and youth

Learning support, mentorship, scholarships, professional development, youth leadership, values-based education, digital literacy, and opportunities for young people to participate in service can create both individual opportunity and stronger civic belonging.

### Food, shelter and humanitarian relief

Faith communities have long traditions of feeding those in need. Shared tables — whether expressed through Niyaaz, langar, community kitchens, food banks or other traditions — offer particularly natural points of connection. Collaboration may also extend to emergency assistance, disaster response, shelter, and support for displaced or vulnerable communities.

### Livelihoods, work and dignity

Skills training, self-employment, microbusiness support, interest-free or low-barrier finance, mentoring, market access and economic-justice initiatives can help families move beyond recurrent crisis toward durable self-reliance.

### Community development

Programmes that strengthen local leadership, volunteerism, social connection, women's participation, family resilience and neighbourhood capacity can help communities become active partners in their own development rather than passive recipients of aid.

### Research, mapping and knowledge sharing

Directories, resource mapping, shared learning, research partnerships, and practical systems that connect needs with expertise and resources. Academic knowledge can be strengthened by grassroots experience, while practitioners can benefit from evidence, evaluation and cross-sector learning.

### Dialogue through service

Shared projects that build understanding through action, develop trust over time, and deepen appreciation for one another's traditions and commitments. Working side by side allows people to encounter one another in a setting of common purpose. Differences remain, but they are placed within a larger human relationship. Over time, trust is built not only through what participants say about their traditions, but through what they do together for others.

### Collective voice

Developing alliances and building momentum to organise and be a force for good.

## A record of dialogue and shared service

The work now taking shape through Humrahe Khair has developed through a series of gatherings and relationships over many years.

**2016 · Calcutta, India.** The first Taqreeb conference, *Propagation of Harmonious Relations in Mughal, British and Independent India*, organised with the University of Calcutta, brought together scholars and public figures from across the Shia–Sunni spectrum as well as Hindu and Christian perspectives. [The 2016 conference](taqreeb-2016-conference.html)

**2017 · New Delhi, India.** The second Taqreeb conference, *Ideas of Harmonious Coexistence: Religions and Philosophies of India*, at Jawaharlal Nehru University, brought together scholars, activists and religious leaders from more than twenty cities internationally. The Syedna Khuzaima Qutbuddin Harmony Prize was launched and awarded to His Holiness the Dalai Lama. [The 2017 conference](taqreeb-2017-conference.html)

**2018 · Mumbai, India.** The Ahimsa Interfaith Event, *World Peace through Non-Violence*, at the Bombay Stock Exchange Auditorium. His Holiness Syedna Taher Fakhruddin TUS addressed the gathering, and the Harmony Prize was presented to H.H. Acharya Dr Lokesh Muni. [The Ahimsa event](story-ahimsa-interfaith-event.html)

**2024 · Boston, USA.** A Muslim intrafaith gathering with His Holiness Syedna Taher Fakhruddin TUS strengthened relationships and renewed discussion about how Muslim communities can preserve their values, support younger generations, engage constructively with wider society, and serve beyond their own boundaries.

**2025 · Boston, USA.** Participants from different Muslim communities came together to listen, learn, map areas of shared concern, and consider how service could become a practical basis for stronger relationships. The emphasis was on moving from goodwill toward sustained cooperation.

**2025 · Thane, Mumbai, India.** The major Humrahe Khair conference brought together faith leaders, nonprofit organisations, philanthropists, academics, grassroots activists, professionals and service practitioners to address poverty and its root causes through cross-sector learning and partnership. Discussions focused on food security, livelihoods, education, health, equity and practical next steps. [Humrahe Khair 2025](hek-2025.html)

**2026 · Boston, USA.** A further gathering in the Boston area, with online participation, continued the conversation and deepened interest in practical collaboration. Participants considered existing resources, unmet needs, volunteer engagement, organisational connections, and ways to create more durable infrastructure for working together.

## The path ahead

### Community Atlas

A long-term priority: a practical resource map of organisations, programmes, expertise, service areas and needs. Over time, this can help participants understand the wider humanitarian landscape, reduce duplication, identify gaps, and connect people more effectively. It can also guide Zahra Hasanaat's own outreach, by showing where existing capacity is strong and where new points of contact may be most useful.

### Points of Light

Points of Light is envisioned as a way to identify and share examples of meaningful service across communities and regions. Making good work visible can inspire participation, spread practical ideas, and reveal opportunities for collaboration. It reflects a core Humrahe Khair instinct: begin by recognising what already exists, and then connect it.

### Volunteer and partnership infrastructure

Clearer pathways for volunteers and organisations to work together — identifying suitable projects, matching skills with needs, supporting local leadership, and developing relationships that continue beyond a single event. Professional volunteers, community ambassadors, mentors, and people who have themselves benefited from programmes can all become part of this network.

### Future convenings

Gatherings in India, the United States and online will continue to bring together religious leaders, humanitarian organisations, researchers, civic leaders, professionals and volunteers, each aiming to deepen relationships while moving toward practical, sustainable collaboration.

For programmes that facilitate greater understanding and opportunities for dialogue across different groups.
