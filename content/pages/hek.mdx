Humrahe Khair, meaning Together for Good, is our platform for cross-community collaboration and shared service. It creates spaces where leaders, educators, NGO professionals and community members can meet, identify common challenges and develop cooperative responses together. Since January 2025 the programme has grown from local conversations into a wider network of gatherings and partnerships spanning India and the USA.

## Vision

Humrahe Khair aims to be a trusted network in which people and organisations learn from one another, share ideas and resources, respond to needs, and strengthen their collective capacity to serve — honouring the distinct identities of participants while cultivating a shared ethic of human dignity, compassion and responsibility.

In a time when division is commonplace, Humrahe Khair unites people and organisations who believe in human dignity above all else — forming a collective force for good that closes gaps, shares resources, and walks alongside communities in service.

## Founder and philosophy

His Holiness Syedna Taher Fakhruddin Saheb, the 54th Dai al-Mutlaq, founded Humrahe Khair in 2025. He was personally trained in Islamic sciences, Fatimid philosophy and the heritage of his predecessors by his father, His Holiness Syedna Khuzaima Qutbuddin Saheb, founder of Zahra Hasanaat. With hands-on experience in finance and agricultural development, he founded and led a profitable hedge fund in the USA in the early 2000s and later built a commercially viable, industrial-scale fruit farming model in California. He brings renewed energy to the office of Dai al-Mutlaq, combining deep Islamic scholarship with an astute understanding of finance and trade.

Syedna Fakhruddin teaches that serving humanity is a sacred responsibility and is an advocate for "human solidarity". He encourages collaboration in humanitarian service and believes there is no dichotomy between preserving and respecting distinct identities and traditions, and living in harmony and helping each other. Taqreeb is the foundation's programme for strengthening relationships within the wider intra-Muslim and interfaith communities and across civic communities, built on the belief that understanding grows through sincere conversation, respectful encounter and practical cooperation. Humrahe Khair emerged from this work as a focused platform for humanitarian collaboration.

Humrahe Khair brings together people and organisations dedicated to humanitarian service, creating opportunities to learn from one another, share ideas and resources, and strengthen collective impact — while identifying gaps, surfacing new opportunities to collaborate, and deepening mutual understanding.

## What we do

### Dialogue gatherings

Structured gatherings bring together leaders, educators, NGO professionals and community members to identify shared challenges, map local resources and shape cooperative responses. Four major convenings took place between January 2025 and April 2026, in Utah, Boston and Mumbai.

### Collaborative service projects

We create opportunities for organisations and volunteers from different communities to work side by side on social service projects, building trust through practical action rather than conversation alone.

### The Community Atlas

A resource-mapping initiative that documents organisations, identifies gaps and highlights opportunities for collaboration across communities. A prototype is in development, alongside an organisational directory and supporting websites.

### Partnerships and networks

We build long-term partnerships with academic institutions, NGOs, civic groups and social-impact leaders, and connect participants to wider networks of expertise, organisations and resources.

### Focus groups and follow-up

From 2026 to 2027 the programme is launching focus groups in India and the USA and formalising volunteer and partnership pathways, so that each gathering leads to sustained cooperation rather than a one-off event.

## Our approach

Humrahe Khair follows a dual-focus strategy. In the short term, structured gatherings reduce isolation between communities and build trust between organisations; in the long term, the programme invests in durable infrastructure — resource mapping, volunteer networks and organisational directories — so that collaboration continues between events. Each convening feeds into practical follow-up, with participants moving from dialogue towards resource-sharing, joint projects and lasting partnerships, while every participating community keeps its own distinct identity and traditions.

## Areas of collaboration

### Research, mapping and knowledge sharing

Directories, resource mapping, shared learning, research partnerships, and practical systems that connect needs with expertise and resources.

### Dialogue through service

Shared projects that build understanding through action, develop trust over time, and deepen appreciation for one another's traditions and commitments.

### Collective voice

Developing alliances and building momentum to organise and be a force for good.

## The path ahead

### Community Atlas

A long-term priority: a practical resource map of organisations, programmes, expertise, service areas and needs. Over time, this can help participants understand the wider humanitarian landscape, reduce duplication, identify gaps, and connect people more effectively.

### Volunteer and partnership infrastructure

Clearer pathways for volunteers and organisations to work together — identifying suitable projects, matching skills with needs, supporting local leadership, and developing relationships that continue beyond a single event.

### Future convenings

Gatherings in India, the United States and online will continue to bring together religious leaders, humanitarian organisations, researchers, civic leaders, professionals and volunteers, each aiming to deepen relationships while moving toward practical, sustainable collaboration.

### Grants and sponsorship

For programmes that facilitate greater understanding and opportunities for dialogue across different groups.
