Zahra Hasanaat was founded in Mumbai in 1996 on a simple principle: enquire about the wellbeing of others — do not wait for them to come to you; go to them, ask how they are, and be helpful in their time of need.

Three decades on, that principle has grown into an integrated model of community development. We work alongside families facing poverty, hunger, educational inequity, poor access to healthcare and fragile livelihoods — not through isolated interventions, but through locally rooted community centres, seven connected program verticals, and the long-term relationships that make lasting change possible.

## Guiding principles

The ultimate aim of everything Zahra Hasanaat does is not only to provide aid but to help individuals and families achieve self-reliance. Upliftment, not charity, benefits both givers and receivers: it builds connectedness, capability and dignity.

We interact with every person with respect and compassion, ensuring the dignity of those receiving help is maintained. Enrolment in our programs is deliberately light-touch — no one should have to prove desperation to receive support.

The organisation is committed to good governance, transparency, fair management, responsible use of funds, and accountability. A governing board oversees stewardship, approvals and systems, supported by dedicated teams for fundraising, communications, and financial budgets and audits.

## How we work

<CoverCard>
### The centre model

Each Zahra Hasanaat centre is a grassroots hub — a welcoming public space where people access services, build relationships and gain skills, shaped by its own neighbourhood and shared across a growing network. Centres begin with one or two strong entry points, such as meals or tuition, and grow into fuller community platforms over time.
</CoverCard>

<CoverCard>
### Seven connected verticals

Nutrition, medical care, education, livelihoods, wellness, community outreach and cross-community collaboration are designed to reinforce one another, so a family is supported through connected channels rather than fragmented service lines.
</CoverCard>

<CoverCard>
### People as infrastructure

Counsellors, outreach workers and more than 100 volunteers are the human system through which trust and continuity are built — treated as essential, never peripheral.
</CoverCard>
