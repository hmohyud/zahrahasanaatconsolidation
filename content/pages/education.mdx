Our Education programme exists to close the gap between the education children receive and the education they deserve. It creates spaces — physical and relational — where children from underserved families can grow in language, creativity and confidence. Built on years of tuition classes and scholarship support for more than 100 students, the programme now spans a community library, English language learning, creative expression workshops, vocational training for women and computer literacy classes.

## What we do

### Community Library

A free multilingual library of around 2,000 books in Marathi, Hindi and English, with informal floor seating designed for children and space for up to 50 readers. Reading circles and storytelling sessions nurture a culture of reading for joy, and children use the space for independent self-study before and after classes.

### English Language Learning

Structured, child-centred English sessions that offer an experiential alternative to rote instruction, drawing on Waldorf education principles. The first cohort completed 18 session days with 88 per cent average attendance, and an adapted curriculum for adult learners is in development.

### Creative Expression Workshops

Regular workshops in art, dance and theatre give children a joyful outlet for self-expression and confidence building, co-facilitated by teachers, librarians and volunteers.

### Vocational Training for Women

Stitching and mehndi classes teach practical, income-generating skills, with flexible scheduling designed around the household responsibilities many women carry.

### Computer Literacy

Foundational digital skills classes for young people and adults, using the computer stations at the centre.

### Tuition and Scholarship Support

Long-running tuition classes and scholarship support that have reached more than 100 students.

## Our approach

We start from the belief that learning is transformative only when it is safe, joyful and deeply human. Sessions are experiential and child-centred rather than rote-based, organised in short modular units so that children facing unstable home circumstances can keep participating, and supported by home visits and family engagement that build the trust which brings learners through the door. Teachers track attendance, language progress and confidence over time, and children who need wider help are connected to counselling, health and nutrition support through our other programmes. Particular care goes into removing barriers to participation for girls and women.
