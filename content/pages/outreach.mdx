Community Outreach is the connective tissue of Zahra Hasanaat's work — how trust is built before services are offered, and how families are seen before they are helped. Rather than arriving with aid or assessment forms, our teams lead with art and listening, returning month after month to the same neighbourhoods so that deeper support becomes possible. The programme works through three strands: neighbourhood outreach visits, a dedicated counsellor team, and a supervised volunteer network.

## What we do

### Neighbourhood art visits

Monthly visits of around an hour and a half to underserved neighbourhoods (bastis), built around a participatory art activity — Build Our Dream Community, using cloth, chalk or paper — that brings children, adults and community workers together. Each visit opens with an icebreaker and closes with shared reflection.

### Home visits

For families who have engaged with the programme or asked for deeper support, team members make individual home visits to understand each household's circumstances more fully and prepare tailored support proposals.

### Counsellor-led family follow-up

A dedicated counsellor team keeps in regular, proactive contact with every family in the organisation's network — asking after their wellbeing rather than waiting to be asked. A structured monthly follow-up tracks family welfare, children's schooling and health, and progress towards goals set with each household.

### Connecting families to wider support

When a need is identified, the team prepares a support proposal that is reviewed by counsellor leads before any assistance is arranged, with monthly follow-up afterwards. Outreach links families into the organisation's nutrition, medical, education and financial programmes, and assistance is connected to positive steps such as school attendance and health check-ups.

### Volunteer training and participation

Every volunteer completes a training workshop covering deep listening, role clarity and ethical boundaries before joining fieldwork. Volunteers support neighbourhood visits, ration distribution, storytelling sessions at the community library, and medical and education camps.

### Safeguarding and accountability

All volunteers and social workers sign a formal agreement covering scope of work, safeguarding, child protection and confidentiality, and work within a clear reporting structure from family to volunteer to coordinator to organisation.

## Our approach

The model is deliberately phased: it begins with open community visits led by art and listening, moves to individual home visits as trust grows, and only then designs tailored support — in recognition that lasting upliftment is only possible when families trust the people who come to help them. A counsellor team maintains each relationship over time through structured monthly follow-up, so that no family falls through the cracks. Trained, supervised volunteers provide the consistent human presence that makes this possible, and every touchpoint can connect a family to nutrition, medical, education or livelihoods support.
