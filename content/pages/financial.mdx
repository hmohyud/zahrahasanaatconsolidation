Poverty is not a fixed condition — it is a circumstance that can change when people are met with genuine support, not charity alone. Our Grants, Loans & Livelihoods programme provides needs-assessed grants and interest-free loans, guided by careful assessment and an ongoing relationship with each family. The goal is not dependency but movement: from crisis towards stability, and from stability towards self-reliance.

## What we do

### Community grants

Needs-assessed grants for medical emergencies, medicines, school fees, food rations and household essentials, and significant life events — including support for widows, debt restructuring and home improvement. Urgent cases are fast-tracked so help arrives when it is needed.

### Interest-free loans

Small loans for business capital, goods and house purchase, disbursed entirely without interest. Repayments are reinvested into new loans, building a revolving fund that allows the programme to sustain itself and reach more families over time.

### Assessment and case management

Every family receives a comprehensive assessment by a counsellor or social worker — including home visits — before any aid decision. Each family is then assigned a named case worker who reviews their progress on a six-month cycle, so support is a continuing relationship rather than a one-time transaction.

### Livelihoods and economic empowerment

Grant and loan recipients are connected to our wider programmes — education, health, nutrition and vocational training — so that financial support is one thread in a family's larger journey towards independence.

### A fair, structured process

Applications come through a central intake office and an online application portal, with every case documented and every decision recorded. Support levels are structured and consistent, so assistance is allocated fairly and reaches the families who genuinely need it most.

## Our approach

Assessment comes before assistance: a counsellor or social worker visits and assesses every family before any aid is disbursed, because home visits consistently surface needs that an application form alone cannot show. Each family then has a single named point of contact who stays with them through six-month review cycles, which we have found builds trust and encourages families to take up complementary support such as counselling, education and vocational training. Loans carry no interest, and repayments seed new loans — so the programme is designed to become self-sustaining rather than dependent on continuous fundraising.
