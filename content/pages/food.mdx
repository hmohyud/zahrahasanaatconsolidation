Food is often where a family's relationship with Zahra Hasanaat begins. Since 2018 our kitchens have served more than 698,000 meals — over 850 every day — alongside structured ration support that eases pressure on household budgets between cycles. We are building this work into a single, dignified food support system that brings together cooked meals, grocery support, home delivery and community giving.

## What we do

### Daily cooked meals

Our kitchens prepare and serve more than 850 hot meals every day, on site and through outreach distribution — immediate hunger relief offered in a spirit of welcome.

### Household ration support

Registered households receive a grocery package every two months, built around staples such as rice, wheat flour, dal, oil, sugar, salt and tea, with a limited choice of add-ons so families take home what they will actually use.

### Home delivery

For elders, people with disabilities, those who are unwell and anyone facing barriers to travel, delivery is a normal part of the service rather than an exception.

### Simple enrolment and scheduling

Families can join through a referral, an outreach visit or a simple WhatsApp message. Intake asks only for the basics, and collection times are confirmed by message — no long queues, and no need to repeat personal hardship at every visit.

### Volunteer and in-kind giving

Volunteers run packing days, delivery routes and intake follow-up, while donation drives gather staples and hygiene items — including seasonal and festival food baskets that have run for the past three years.

### Nutrition quality

With guidance from volunteer medical and public-health advisers, we are shaping food baskets towards better dietary balance, with child- and women-sensitive items where possible and practical household guidance on healthier everyday meals.

## Our approach

The programme is designed so that no one has to prove desperation in order to eat. Enrolment is light-touch, collection works by appointment rather than queue, packaging is neat and discreet, and home delivery is standard for those who cannot travel. This matters because many vulnerable families are excluded from formal ration systems by documentation gaps or administrative barriers — a community programme should close that gap, not repeat it. Food support also serves as a doorway, connecting families to education, health and livelihood support across our other programmes.
